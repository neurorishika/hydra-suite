    Post-processing¶

    Post-processing is where HYDRA Suite turns raw online trajectories into something you can trust for analysis.

    The current implementation is deliberately conservative: it would rather keep two defensible fragments than one polished but incorrect identity trace.

    Why Post-processing Exists¶

    Online tracking has to make decisions immediately. That is useful for visualization and throughput, but it also means difficult events can slip through:

    • crossings,
    • temporary occlusions,
    • missing detections,
    • abrupt detector failures,
    • and short identity swaps.

    Post-processing gets a second chance to inspect those events with more context.

    Current Processing Stages¶

    1. Remove weak fragments¶

    Very short trajectories are dropped using MIN_TRAJECTORY_LENGTH.

    This removes:

    • detector noise,
    • unstable startup segments,
    • and partial fragments that are too small to support reliable identity claims.

    2. Split implausible motion¶

    The pipeline can break a trajectory when motion becomes physically implausible.

    Controls:

    • MAX_VELOCITY_BREAK
    • MAX_VELOCITY_ZSCORE
    • VELOCITY_ZSCORE_WINDOW
    • VELOCITY_ZSCORE_MIN_VELOCITY

    Use the fixed threshold when you know the approximate speed envelope. Use the z-score breaker when failures look like sudden outliers relative to each trajectory's own history.

    3. Split long occlusion runs¶

    If a trajectory stays in occluded state for too long, the current implementation can split at that gap rather than pretending continuity survived a long missing period.

    Control:

    • MAX_OCCLUSION_GAP

    4. Merge forward and backward hypotheses¶

    If backward tracking was enabled, the post-processor compares forward and backward outputs and merges only the segments that genuinely agree.

    Controls:

    • AGREEMENT_DISTANCE
    • MIN_OVERLAP_FRAMES

    5. Stitch broken fragments¶

    After merge resolution, nearby fragments can be stitched across short gaps when the geometry still makes sense.

    This is a pragmatic recovery step for tracks broken by:

    • quick turns,
    • short detector dropouts,
    • or ambiguity during close interactions.

    6. Relink with motion and optional pose continuity¶

    The current relinking step is stronger than simple gap stitching. It summarizes fragment endpoints and asks whether the later fragment could plausibly be the continuation of the earlier one.

    It uses:

    • motion extrapolation,
    • heading compatibility,
    • and pose-shape compatibility when pose data exists.

    Controls:

    • ENABLE_TRACKLET_RELINKING
    • RELINK_POSE_MAX_DISTANCE
    • MAX_OCCLUSION_GAP
    • MAX_VELOCITY_BREAK

    7. Interpolate short gaps¶

    Interpolation is the last stage. It is not intended to rescue fundamentally bad tracking.

    Controls:

    • INTERPOLATION_METHOD
    • INTERPOLATION_MAX_GAP

    Available modes:

    • none
    • linear
    • cubic
    • spline

    Recommended Defaults¶

    For most experiments, a safe default posture is:

    • enable post-processing,
    • keep merge thresholds conservative,
    • enable relinking only when fragmenting is a real problem,
    • use linear interpolation with a small max gap,
    • and visually spot-check crossings before trusting derived statistics.

    When to Be More Conservative¶

    Tighten the post-processing stack if:

    • animals are visually similar and identity errors are costly,
    • social interactions create frequent overlaps,
    • or downstream metrics depend heavily on exact individual continuity.

    When to Be More Permissive¶

    Relax it only when:

    • detections are clean,
    • crossings are rare,
    • and fragmentation is the main failure mode.

    Identity Evidence and the Quality Breaker¶

    When identity is enabled, the fragment solver can split a trajectory where the identity evidence changes (PELT changepoint detection) before assigning labels. Two behaviours are worth knowing:

    • The PELT penalty is in probability units. The signal is the smoothed per-label posterior on the simplex, [0, 1], used raw. Earlier versions z-scored it per trajectory, which made the penalty's units depend on the trajectory and inflated float noise on a flat posterior into unit-variance "signal" -- 660 spurious splits over 128 tracks in one measured run. Because the units are now absolute, a penalty tuned on one video means the same thing on the next.

    • Uninformative evidence is refused, not acted on. Before splitting, the solver assesses evidence quality: the fraction of detections confident above EVIDENCE_CONF_LEVEL (0.5) must reach EVIDENCE_MIN_CONF_FRAC (10%), and the distinct-labels-per-frame diversity must reach EVIDENCE_MIN_DIVERSITY (0.30) of what is achievable. If either fails, the solver logs an ERROR and refuses to split or assign:

    fragment_solver: identity evidence is uninformative (confident=..% of
detections, diversity=.. over .. frames) -- refusing to split or assign
identities. Check the classifier's fit_policy / preprocessing.

    Trajectories are then left intact rather than shredded by a source that does not know anything. The smoothed per-row posterior is still written to IdentityFinalSmoothedLabel / ...Confidence so you can see what the (uninformative) source thought. Seeing this ERROR almost always means the classifier is being fed the wrong crop shape -- see Older Classifiers and Crop Preprocessing.

    Honest Identity Evidence¶

    Three behaviours keep the fragment solver from treating weak identity evidence as strong. All three are on by default; the last two ship as part of the evidence-trust package below (see How the defaults were chosen):

    • Non-identifying mass stays "unknown". When a classifier puts probability on a class that is not in the identity catalog -- a declared non-identifying class such as notag, or anything out of vocabulary -- that probability now goes to the catalog's unknown slot. Before, it was dropped and the remainder renormalized, so a crop that was 90 % notag and 10 % one tag read as a confident tag. Config key identity_noncatalog_to_unknown (engine IDENTITY_NONCATALOG_TO_UNKNOWN, default true). It applies to the realtime decoder and the offline solver alike.
    • Absolute support gate. fragment_min_support (default 0.5) is a floor on relative support -- support renormalized over the known labels, which saturates towards 1 on any long trajectory whatever the absolute evidence. fragment_min_absolute_posterior (engine FRAGMENT_MIN_ABSOLUTE_POSTERIOR, default 0.3, 0 = off) additionally requires the fragment's mean absolute posterior on a label (the unknown mass kept in the denominator) to reach this value before the label is a candidate at all. It only applies to fragments that have cache evidence; a fragment with none keeps whatever the online prior supports.
    • Absolute seeding. Fragments claim labels in descending duration x evidence order; with fragment_absolute_seeding (engine FRAGMENT_ABSOLUTE_SEEDING, default true) the evidence term is the absolute posterior, so a long weakly-identified trajectory no longer claims a label ahead of a shorter, clearly-identified one.

    fragment_length_weight has always been clamped to [0, 1]; a value outside that range now logs a warning instead of being clamped silently.

    Evidence-Trust Layer¶

    identity_evidence_trust (engine IDENTITY_EVIDENCE_TRUST, default true) decides, from detection geometry alone -- never from the classifier -- which evidence frames show one animal clearly, and lets those drive identity:

    1. Trusted frames. A detection-backed frame is trusted when all active checks pass:
    2. isolation: the nearest other detection centre in the same frame is more than identity_trust_isolation_bl (1.0) body lengths away;
    3. single-animal shape: OBB area within +-identity_trust_area_tolerance (0.35) of the single-animal prior, and elongation at least identity_trust_min_aspect_fraction (0.65) of the prior's;
    4. keypoint completeness (only when the run has pose): at least identity_trust_min_keypoint_fraction (0.75) of keypoints with confidence >= identity_trust_keypoint_conf (0.5).
    5. Per-video calibration. The body length and the shape prior are the medians over this video's isolated detections (nearest neighbour more than identity_trust_prior_isolation_bl, 2.0, body lengths away) -- a whole-video median is dominated by touching animals in a crowded video. The shape check switches itself off when fewer than identity_trust_min_prior_detections (200) isolated detections exist, the keypoint check when there is no pose, and the whole layer when the detection cache (the geometry source) is unavailable. There is no stationarity filter.
    6. Distinct views. Consecutive trusted frames collapse into one view until the animal moves identity_trust_view_move_bl (0.5) body lengths or turns identity_trust_view_turn_deg (30) degrees, so an object seen 10 000 times standing still counts once.
    7. Tiered solving. Fragments with at least identity_trust_min_views (1) trusted views form the trusted tier: their support is the mean absolute posterior over views, a label is a candidate when that absolute support reaches identity_trust_min_support (0.3; fragment_min_support stays the relative floor of the untiered path), and they all claim labels first (ordered by views x support). Every other fragment uses the honest evidence above, seeds after the trusted tier, and may never displace a trusted fragment -- it can only take labels the trusted tier left free. With identity_trust_top_label_only (default true; only read when the trust layer is on) a fragment is only ever given its own best label: if another fragment holds that label it stays unknown rather than taking its runner-up. The runner-up is usually a lookalike tag (blue_yellow for green_yellow) whose real owner is elsewhere in the arena.
    8. Splitting on trusted switches. With enable_pelt_splitting on and the trust layer active, PELT on every frame's smoothed posterior is replaced by a split at confident label switches in the trusted-view sequence: label A on one side, B on the other, each side at least identity_trust_split_min_views (3) views with mean posterior >= identity_trust_split_min_confidence (0.5), and the two-label explanation better than the best single label by identity_trust_split_min_log_odds (8.0) nats of evidence. The cut is placed in the untrusted gap between the two views. With the trust layer off, enable_pelt_splitting keeps the original PELT behaviour.
    9. Splitting at contacts. Identity swaps happen while animals touch. With identity_trust_contact_split (default true; runs whenever the trust layer is active, independent of enable_pelt_splitting) a trajectory is cut at a contact only when the evidence on the two sides of it names different animals. A row is in contact when the nearest other detection is closer than identity_trust_contact_bl (0.5) body lengths, or when it has no detection (occluded). Contact runs are found with gaps of up to identity_trust_contact_fill (2) rows bridged and runs shorter than identity_trust_contact_min_frames (3) rows ignored. For each run, up to identity_trust_contact_window (30) clean, non-contact evidence frames on each side (never past the neighbouring contacts) are compared with the same three tests as step 5 (identity_trust_split_min_views frames per side, identity_trust_split_min_confidence, identity_trust_split_min_log_odds). The contact run itself becomes its own short piece. A contact with too little clean evidence on a side is left alone, so the trajectory keeps its motion-based continuity there, and cuts whose two sides end with the same label are rejoined after assignment.

    Each run logs one summary line: trusted fraction, body length, prior, and which checks were active, plus the number of contact-switch cuts.

    TrackerKit controls (Post-processing → Advanced options → Identity Post-Processing → Fragment Solver): Minimum label confidence (fragment_min_absolute_posterior), Trust identity evidence only from clear single-animal views (identity_evidence_trust), Clear-view distance (identity_trust_isolation_bl), Split at contacts where the identity evidence switches (identity_trust_contact_split), Contact distance (identity_trust_contact_bl) and Split evidence margin (identity_trust_split_min_log_odds, shared by steps 5 and 6). Every other trust key is config-only. The layer was validated together with fragment_min_absolute_posterior: 0.3 and fragment_absolute_seeding: true (the untrusted tier's evidence); all three are on by default, so change them together. When the layer cannot run (no detection geometry) it logs a warning and the solver uses the untiered path; top-label-only then does not apply. Trusted-switch splitting still needs enable_pelt_splitting, which stays off by default: with the layer inactive it falls back to PELT.

    How the defaults were chosen¶

    Each piece was replayed through the real fragment solver on six crowded full-arena videos (central dish labels compared against a dish-only reference) and switched on by default only if, on every video, agreement did not drop, the wrong-label rate did not rise by more than 0.005, and the labels it removed had a median per-frame posterior below 0.3 (i.e. it removed unsupported labels, not real ones).

    • Non-identifying mass to unknown: agreement never lower by more than 0.0001 and up to +0.14 higher, wrong rate never up by more than 0.003. It is a correctness fix and ships on; on one video the few labels it removed (388 rows) had a median posterior of 0.35.
    • Absolute gate + seeding: agreement up on five videos (up to +0.23) but the wrong-label rate rose by 0.011 and 0.007 on two -- left off on its own (it is on now only as part of the trust package).
    • Trust layer: agreement up on all six (up to +0.37) and the wrong-label rate down or within 0.001 on all six, but it also removes labels whose per-frame posterior is moderate (median 0.36-0.61 on four videos) and lowers coverage of crowded regions by up to 6 points -- off by default at the time. Adding trusted-view splitting raises agreement further (0.87-0.97 on five videos, 0.75 on the most crowded) and fixes most relabel-to-the-wrong- animal cases, at the same coverage trade-off.
    • Top label only (trust layer on): rerun through the full pipeline on seven full-arena videos. Wrong-label rate 0.046 -> 0.015 and 0.028 -> 0.015 on the two videos with the most lookalike-label errors, unchanged elsewhere; agreement within 0.004 and crowded-region coverage within 0.6 points on all seven. Default on whenever the trust layer is on.
    • Package default on (2026-11): the trust layer, the absolute gate and absolute seeding are on by default, on the strength of one dataset: 66 full-arena ant videos (central-dish agreement median 0.836 -> 0.936, wrong-label rate 0.022 -> 0.013, chamber coverage unchanged) and 40 blind-proofread segments. Where the trust run and the dish-only reference disagreed, the proofreader sided with the trust run 9 times out of 10 and with the reference never; in the chambers, on segments that were one animal throughout, the trust run was right 5/5 and the old default 0/5. Its cost: most dish rows it leaves unknown were readable (8 of 10). It has not yet been validated on a second dataset.
    • Contact split (2026-11): on the same dataset, every proofread identity swap happened while the animal was within 0.5 body lengths of another. Cutting at every contact caught 8 of 9 swaps but tripled the number of trajectories; cutting only where the evidence switches across the contact cost 1.04-1.10x trajectories. Rerun through the full pipeline on 34 clips and scored row by row against the proofreading, it moved swap rows from 50% correct / 50% wrong to 68% / 32% (two of the five wrongly labelled segments fixed, none newly broken), raised clean rows from 73% to 78% correct with wrong unchanged at 2.2%, and raised whole-video coverage by 1.0 point (dish 92.1% -> 93.1%, dish agreement 91.1% -> 92.3%), at 1.03x trajectories. Treating long pile-ups specially (never carrying identity through them) fixed no further swaps and cost 5-8 points of coverage, so it is not offered.
